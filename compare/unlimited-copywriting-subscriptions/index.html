Unlimited copywriting subscriptions vs project plans

An unlimited plan sells throughput. A project plan sells attention per page. If another company could have written your page, it will not sell for you.

Both models charge a flat monthly fee, run on a shared board and return drafts in about two business days. They differ in who does the strategy, how revisions move and how much work each request gets.

How unlimited plans work

You pay one flat monthly fee and add as many requests as you like to a board in Notion or Trello. The writer works through them one at a time, or two at a time on a higher tier. Each request comes back in about two business days on average. Larger ones take longer or arrive in parts.

Revisions are usually unlimited. Most plans cover blog posts, social posts, emails, web pages, ads and scripts. Some cap the words in a single request and some cap nothing. Most run without meetings and let you pause or cancel any month. Some are one senior writer and some are a team, so ask who will write your requests.

The word unlimited describes the queue, not a promise of finished volume. Output depends on how many requests can be active at once. A request usually stays active until it is complete, so each round of feedback holds the slot and the next request waits.

One at a time

Unlimited plans usually keep one request active. The next one waits, and feedback rounds hold the slot. Ask what counts as a request before you compare it with a project plan.

One request handled both ways

Say you need your pricing page rewritten before you add an enterprise tier. Here is how that request moves through each model.

A writer turns the brief into a clean draft in about two days. You notice it treats enterprise like a bigger self-serve plan, so you add notes. The revision comes back the next day while the blog posts wait.

Check these before you choose

  1. Count the copy requests you shipped last quarter and divide by three. That is your real monthly volume.
  2. Sort them into pages that sell and content that publishes. Homepages, pricing pages, launch pages and nurture emails sell. Blog posts, social posts and newsletters publish.
  3. Name the person who writes your briefs today. If nobody owns positioning, you need the strategy inside the plan.
  4. Check how fast your team reviews drafts. A queue only moves as fast as your feedback.
  5. Decide whether one writer has to know your product on every request.
  6. Pick the one page that brings in the most revenue and ask what a better version is worth.

If most of your answers point to content that publishes, a fast queue will serve you well. If they point to pages that sell, a set number of projects with research behind each one will serve you better.

Ask any provider these questions

  • Who writes my requests, and is it the same person every time?
  • Does a request hold its slot while I review it?
  • Is there a word cap on a single request?
  • What counts as one request when I need a full landing page or a five-email sequence?
  • Does strategy come from my brief, or will you push back on it?
  • What happens to my queue and my rate if I pause?

Which model fits

Pick an unlimited plan if

  • You publish blog posts, social posts or newsletters every week and need steady volume.
  • Your positioning is settled and your briefs already say what each piece should claim.
  • Someone on your team can review drafts within a day, so the queue keeps moving.
  • You send lots of small edits and would rather not count projects.
  • Cost per piece matters more than the depth of research behind each one.

Pick a project plan if

  • Your homepage, pricing page or launch page brings in most of your revenue.
  • Nobody owns messaging and you want a writer who questions the brief.
  • You want the same senior writer on every page.
  • You need several projects moving in the same week, such as during a launch.
  • You would rather approve four strong pages than review ten drafts.

You can also run both. Send blog and social volume to an unlimited plan and keep the pages that sell on a project plan.
